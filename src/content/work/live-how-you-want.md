## The challenge

Live How You Want sells festival travel: hotel packages and shuttle transportation for events such as EDC Las Vegas and EDC Orlando. Demand follows the festival calendar. Interest builds well before an event, and many people start looking before a product is on sale.

That creates two problems. A visitor who arrives early needs something to do other than leave. And once someone does raise a hand, the business needs to know who they are, what they asked about, and what to send them next, without a person copying details between tools.

## My contribution

I worked across the website and the systems behind it. On the front end, I developed festival travel landing pages on Bubble and improved the paths that lead to hotel and shuttle products. Behind the pages, I built lead capture and waitlist experiences and connected them to CRM workflows in GoHighLevel. I also produced and updated search-driven content and supported the marketing automation and guest communications for EDC Las Vegas and EDC Orlando campaigns.

## The solution

I treated the journey as one system with four stages.

1. **Discovery.** Content written around what festival travelers search for, pointing to the landing page for the relevant event.
2. **Landing page.** One page per event that explains the hotel and shuttle options and makes the next step obvious.
3. **Inquiry, waitlist or reservation.** When a product is available, the page leads into the booking journey. When it is not, a waitlist form captures interest.
4. **Follow-up.** Each submission creates or updates a contact in the CRM, and a workflow takes over from there.

The point of connecting these stages is that no lead depends on someone remembering to act on it.

## Technical implementation

The landing pages and booking journey live in Bubble. Forms on those pages pass contact details and the event of interest into GoHighLevel, where the contact is tagged by event and product. Those tags decide which workflow runs: a waitlist confirmation, an update when a product opens, or communications for guests who have reserved.

Email campaigns run from the same CRM, so a message about EDC Orlando shuttles goes to people who asked about EDC Orlando shuttles. This is the kind of setup I describe under [GoHighLevel CRM and marketing automation](/services/gohighlevel/): the workflow is designed around the business process first, then built.

On the content side, I updated pages so that titles, headings and copy match how people look for festival travel, and linked related pages to each other. The approach follows the same principles as my [technical SEO work](/services/technical-seo/).

## Outcome

What now exists:

- Festival travel landing pages for EDC Las Vegas and EDC Orlando campaigns.
- Clearer paths from those pages to hotel and shuttle products.
- Waitlist and lead capture forms that feed the CRM directly.
- Workflows that tag contacts and handle confirmations and campaign emails.
- Search-driven content that is kept up to date.

I have not published performance figures for this project, and I do not attribute revenue to any single part of it.

## Considerations

Festival products change every season: dates, hotels, routes and availability. I built pages and workflows that can be updated for the next event without being rebuilt, which meant keeping the structure simple and the tagging consistent.

Working across two platforms has a cost. Bubble and GoHighLevel each hold part of the customer record, so the handoff between them has to be tested whenever a form or a workflow changes. A waitlist is also a promise. If people sign up and hear nothing, the form has done harm, so the follow-up message was written before the form went live.
